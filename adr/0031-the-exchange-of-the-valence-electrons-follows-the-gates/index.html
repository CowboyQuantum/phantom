    0031 — The exchange of the dots' valence electrons follows the gates: the singlet secular equation on the CI window's local sector at the gated coupling and the shaped dots; the Pauli readout at the gated coupling

    Status: Accepted Date: 2026-10-31 Step: 8 follow-up (issue #60, the last of #58–#60; after ADR 0029 and 0030)

    Context

    After ADR 0029 and 0030 the barrier gates set the interdot coupling and the dots' shape, but not the exchange. State.exchange_ev came from the two-site Hubbard model (ADR 0021). That model holds 0–2 electrons per dot and took t from the barrier gate alone, with no ceiling. A compiled device's dots hold about 23 electrons each, so away from the Hubbard model's own symmetric point J described an unrelated charge configuration. At the reference device's programmed point it read about 13 meV, whether t was 0.14 or 45 µeV. The Pauli spin blockade (PSB) readout used the compiled t, not the t at the gates. The bisection behind J also divided zero by zero where t underflowed: a RuntimeWarning, with a finite J.

    The owner asked for the barrier gate to set the exchange frequency (2026-10-31).

    Three forces shaped the design:

    • Precision. J spans twelve decades. The secular equation solved relative to E(1,1) keeps its relative digits (ADR 0021), and the charge gaps have to keep theirs.
    • Consistency. The CI window already carries the gated t (ADR 0029) and the shaped capacitances (ADR 0030).
    • Scope. The spin layer's protocols and the coherence optimum use typed or few-electron Hubbard models at their symmetric point, which sits far from the operating point. There, a t extrapolated from the operating point's slopes would mean nothing.

    Decision

    J at a gate point is the singlet–triplet splitting of the dots' valence electrons, from the two-electron secular equation on the CI window's local sector. Specifically:

    • The sector. The reference is the ground charge state (N₁, N₂) of the hybridized window. The partners are (N₁+1, N₂−1), Δ_L above, and (N₁−1, N₂+1), Δ_R above, at the CI energies at the gates.
    • Each gap is formed directly from N − n_g, as E(δᵀC⁻¹(N − n_g) + ½δᵀC⁻¹δ) with C = C(V), so it keeps its digits.
    • A partner with a negative occupation is left out (Δ = +inf).
    • t is the gated coupling.
    • spin.exchange.local_exchange_ev; the secular solver is shared (singlet_exchange_ev). Where t = 0 it returns J = −min(0, Δ_R, Δ_L) without dividing zero by zero.
    • Meaning. This is the exchange of a qubit pair where both dots hold an odd number of electrons: the effective (1,1). Elsewhere the valence spins are paired, J describes no qubit, and it stays finite. Like the Hubbard model, the sector has spinless orbitals and one valley.
    • The consumer surface.
    • State.exchange_ev is this J, and State.exchange_frequency_hz = J/h.
    • Session.spin_readout reads at the interdot coupling of the last point the session was given.
    • The few-electron Hubbard models, runtime_hubbard and the coherence optimum keep the barrier-gate exponential at their symmetric point.
    • Release. 0.4.0. Model versions: spin.exchange 3, device.session 7.

    Consequences

    • Easier:
    • The barrier gate sets the exchange frequency. On the reference device's (23, 28) cell, J/h goes from about 1 kHz with B_M 50 mV below the programmed point to 230 MHz at it and to tens of GHz as B_M opens. It follows 2t²(1/Δ_R + 1/Δ_L) while t ≪ Δ and falls below it as t nears the gaps.
    • Across an odd–odd cell, J rises toward both anticrossings (exchange-frequency-vs-barrier).
    • The readout's contrast closes with the barrier.
    • Harder:
    • Jumps. J jumps where the ground charge state changes, because its reference sector changes with it. Inside a cell it is smooth.
    • No spin structure. There are no valleys or orbital excitations in the sector, so for multi-electron valence shells J is the single-orbital estimate.
    • Two exchanges. The Hubbard models in the spin layer still give the few-electron J of ADR 0021 at their symmetric point, so a device's State.exchange_ev and optimum(device).coherence describe different charge configurations.
    • Implausibly large J. t inherits the unscreened sensitivity of #48, so J reaches tens of GHz within tens of mV.
    • Must now:
    • A consumer reads J and J/h per point.
    • A consumer reads the effective (1,1) from the parity of n_ground.

    Alternatives considered

    • Hubbard models placed at the device's valence electrons per point. Rejected: a 16×16 diagonalisation per point, the same physics as the secular equation, and it loses J's digits (ADR 0021).
    • J as NaN outside odd–odd cells. Rejected: the contract requires State to be finite. The parity is available from n_ground.
    • The spin layer's sweet spot and gate fidelity on the device's local sector. Deferred. It would move the coherence optimum to the operating point's cell, a change to a signed-off-class protocol family, and should be its own issue.
    • The compiled t in the readout. Rejected: the owner asked for the readout to follow the barrier gates too.
