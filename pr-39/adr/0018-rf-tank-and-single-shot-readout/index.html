    0018 — RF reflectometry as a lumped tank loaded by the sensor; single-shot readout as integrated noisy shots

    Status: Accepted Date: 2026-10-26 Step: 4

    Context

    Step 4 owes the readout response beyond the DC sensor current: the radio-frequency reflectometry that real setups use for fast charge readout, and the single-shot statistics (histograms, signal-to-noise, fidelity against integration time) that decide whether a readout works. The step file's open decision was how much of RF to model: a transfer function or the tank plus sensor impedance.

    Decision

    • RF (readout.rf.TankCircuit): a series inductor into the sensor's small-bias resistance in parallel with a parasitic capacitance, driven through a 50 Ω line. Γ = (Z − Z₀)/(Z + Z₀) with Z = iωL + 1/(iωC_p + 1/R). This gives the resonance, the matching resistance L/(C_p Z₀) at which the reflection vanishes, and the phase flip across it, from three numbers. The sensor resistance is V_sd / I of the SET model, so the RF flank mirrors the DC Coulomb peak. Cables, amplifier noise and a tunable match are not modelled; a consumer that needs them puts a transfer function in front.
    • Single shot (readout.single_shot): a shot integrates the sensor current for τ at a readout point with the double dot in one of two charge states; shots are consecutive in time on the device's noise processes, so 1/f drift and telegraph switching move the distributions between shots as they do in a real histogram. White readout noise keeps a fixed spectral density: its per-sample standard deviation scales as 1/√dt from the 15 pA stated at 1 ms sampling, so its shot contribution falls as 1/√τ. SNR is the separation of the two means over the pooled standard deviation; fidelity is the fraction of shots on the correct side of the midpoint threshold.
    • Protocols: rf-resonance, rf-sensor-flank, single-shot-histogram, snr-vs-integration-time (the last shows the √τ law and its breakdown when slow noise takes over).

    Consequences

    • Easier: RF and DC readout share one sensor model; the SNR curve exposes the noise model's low-frequency content directly, which the P1 charge-noise row will constrain.
    • Harder: amplifier noise temperature, the dominant limit in practice, is absent; the SNR here is the device-side bound. The noise amplitudes are placeholders (calibration: none).
    • Must now: the catalog entry for traps and fluctuators (#1) sets the telegraph and 1/f parameters the histogram depends on; until then the picture is a placeholder shape.

    Alternatives considered

    • A transfer function only — rejected: matching and the phase flip are what a tune-up looks for, and they need the impedance.
    • Full circuit with cable and amplifier — deferred; adds parameters without changing the device physics.
