    0032 — The interdot conductance sets the triple-point splitting: Kemble's transmission of the interdot barrier, Golden & Halperin's f(g), an effective interdot capacitance in the CI model

    Status: Accepted Date: 2026-10-31 Step: 8 follow-up (issue #66; after ADR 0029–0031)

    Context

    After #58–#60 the owner still did not see the behaviour they were tuning for: "if I increase the barrier gate between plunger gates, the interdot width (in anti-detuning) should get bigger until I form a superdot underneath the barrier gate. The opposite should also be true, a lower barrier gate voltage should shrink the interdot width until all I see is a grid" (2026-10-31). That width is the triple points' separation. On the reference device, the fractional splitting f = 2C_m/(C_t + C_m) moved only from 0.14 to 0.16 over ±0.2 V of B_M:

    • Geometry. C_m came from the sphere formula of ADR 0013, which B_M changed by about 0.7 aF.
    • Hybridization. ADR 0029's coupling rounds the interdot line but shifts the triple points by at most 2t/E_add ≈ 0.15.
    • No superdot. A single tunnelling level cannot produce the merged dot at all.

    In double-dot transport the splitting follows the interdot conductance through quantum charge fluctuations (Waugh et al. 1995; review: van der Wiel et al. 2003, the honeycomb from a grid to one dot). Golden & Halperin give f(g) for N_ch channels of conductance g each, with a weak and a strong limit that do not interpolate reliably for N_ch = 2.

    The owner chose (2026-10-31): - a conductance-driven splitting entering the CI model as an effective interdot capacitance; - a smooth interpolation between the published limits; - folding it into 0.4.0.

    Four forces shaped the design:

    • Continuity to the merge. The conductance has to reach 1 continuously as the dots merge. WKB's e^(−2S) stops at S = 0, and the rates' logistic halves it.
    • Bit-exact limit. Without conductance nothing may change.
    • Gate charge. The tunnelling relaxes charge quantization. It must not move the gates' induced charge.
    • References. Every published curve enters as a proposed reference entry (ADR 0007).

    Decision

    The interdot conductance per channel is Kemble's transmission of the interdot barrier, and Golden & Halperin's f(g) sets an effective interdot capacitance in the CI model. Specifically:

    • The barrier.
    • Compile records the top ΔU of the interdot barrier above its fixed tunnelling level (ADR 0027), negative below it: the highest U on the straight cut between the dots, sampled bilinearly.
    • It also records ΔU's slopes against every gate, with the dots held, and its curvature ħω_b (a parabola fitted ±4 nm around the top). The shape grid gains a barrier_top_ev column, the top with the dots moved.
    • Device fields interdot_barrier_top_ev, interdot_barrier_top_slopes_ev_per_v and interdot_barrier_hbar_omega_ev; DEVICE_FORMAT_VERSION 5.
    • The conductance. g = 1/(1 + exp(2πΔU/ħω_b)) (Kemble), with N_ch = 2 (spin). It is ½ when the level reaches the top, and it goes continuously towards 1 as the barrier sinks to the merge.
    • The splitting.
    • f_GH(g) joins (2 ln 2/π²)·N_ch·g (g ≤ 0.15) and 1 + (16e^γ/π³)(1 − g)·ln(1 − g) (g ≥ 0.85) with a monotone cubic (calibration: none).
    • The total is f = f_C + (F_max − f_C)·f_GH(g), with f_C = 2C_m/(C_t + C_m) of the geometric capacitances and F_max = 0.98.
    • The CI model.
    • ConstantInteractionParams.splitting adds ΔC_m·[[1, −1], [−1, 1]] to the capacitances in the energies, with ΔC_m = (F_max − f_C)·f_GH·(C_t + C_m)/(2(1 − f)). That is exactly zero at g = 0.
    • The gate-induced charge keeps the geometric capacitances.
    • The CI model's measured splitting along the symmetric axis is f (P0).
    • The consumer surface. State.interdot_conductance and State.interdot_splitting; Device.runtime_splitting().
    • References. golden1996-peak-splitting, waugh1995-tunable-coupling and kemble1935-wkb-barrier as proposed entries, and the honeycomb evolution as an item of vanderwiel2003-double-dot-transport.
    • Release. 0.4.0. Model versions: charge.splitting 1, charge.shape 2, compile.device 6, device 7, device.session 8.

    Consequences

    • Easier:
    • Opening B_M takes the honeycomb from small hexagons, through elongated hexagons, to the diagonal lines of one superdot, and closing it shrinks the segments back (honeycomb-vs-barrier).
    • On the reference device g rises from 0.002 at the operating point to 0.99 at B_M +50 mV, and f from 0.16 to 0.94. Where the dots merge, f holds at 0.98.
    • The exchange (ADR 0031) and every other CI quantity see the same capacitances.
    • Harder:
    • No exact grid. Closing B_M shrinks f only to the geometric f_C ≈ 0.14. The sphere-formula C_m has no screening by the barrier gate (#48), so the "grid" is small hexagons, not a grid.
    • Steep rise. f rises within about 40 mV of B_M. Kemble's transmission changes by e for every ħω_b/2π ≈ 0.7 meV of barrier height, and the unscreened barrier moves fast with the gates (#48).
    • The f(g) curve. Between g = 0.15 and 0.85 it is an interpolation, which the authors themselves call unreliable for N_ch = 2. A digitised measurement (Waugh et al.) would replace it as a P1 row.
    • Mixed models. The splitting is the metallic-dot theory (many levels) applied to semiconductor dots of about 23 electrons. It sits on top of ADR 0029's single-level hybridization, and the two overlap by at most 2t/E_add.
    • Asymmetry. For unequal dots f uses the mean total capacitance (the symmetric-equivalent definition).
    • Must now:
    • Device files from earlier 0.4.0 commits do not load (format 5).
    • A test that checks the shape's own capacitances turns the splitting off.

    Alternatives considered

    • A better electrostatic C_m from the potential (overlapping charge densities). Rejected by the owner. It is closer to the geometry, but it cannot reach the superdot without a solver, and it moves f little.
    • Fitting measured f(g) (Waugh, Livermore) now. Deferred. The curve has to be digitised and confirmed by a human before it can be the standard (ADR 0007).
    • The WKB transmission e^(−2S), or the rates' logistic, as the conductance. Rejected. Either stops at a half (or at one, abruptly) when the barrier falls below the level, so f would jump at the merge.
    • Raising t's ceiling, or several levels per dot. Rejected. The single-level splitting is 2t, and the merged dot needs the many-level charge-fluctuation physics that f(g) summarises.
    • Moving the induced charge with the effective capacitance. Rejected. Tunnelling relaxes charge quantization and does not change how much charge the gates induce.
