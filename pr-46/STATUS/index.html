    Known gaps

    • Owner-dependent, never blocking: sign-offs for all 66 protocols; catalog entries #1–#14; confirmation (or replacement) of the eight proposed reference entries; the papers for the P1 rows of Steps 2–6.
    • The compiled lead and interdot rates are constant over voltage at runtime (State says so); a session's charge dynamics use them everywhere.
    • Compile moves the sensor gate onto its flank in one step and re-derives the dots, so a draw whose charge shifts with the sensor gate lands off the 0.9-width target (seeds 0 and 3: 0.87 and 0.79 widths).
    • spin_readout takes a prepared triplet probability and does not read the session's charge or voltages (#41).
    • A point evaluated alone and inside a batch agree within 1e-12 relative, not bit for bit (numpy's kernels); the contract states it.
    • The clean textbook layout's interdot barrier is high: at B_M = 0.2 V the clean t is 0.03 µeV. Across seeds 0–11 at the gallery operating point the compiled t spans 0.003–900 µeV and seed 6's interface charges stop the sensor dot from forming; the placeholder charge density dominates the spread until catalog entry #1 sets it. The gallery device is seed 2 (t = 52 µeV, lead rates 1e9–1e11 Hz). The barrier gate's exponential scale is 7–10 mV per factor e, a property of the closed-form potential without screening.
    • No screening until the FEM backend exists. Trapping hysteresis (disorder.trapping) is not part of the compiled device.
    • Spin, valley, noise and disorder placeholders (calibration: none) as listed in the step files; the quasi-static detuning noise takes the whole 1/f band; Step 4's lock-in dynamics on the master equation still owed; amplifier noise absent from the SNR picture; the two-dot CI model cannot represent a plunger–barrier short.
    • Cross-platform agreement (ADR 0014, 1e-9 relative): quantities that cancel digits (eigenvalue differences, finite differences, argmax ties on a flat maximum, interpolation through near-equal values) have each broken the Windows gallery comparison once; use closed forms, Hellmann–Feynman derivatives, grid points or secular equations instead.
