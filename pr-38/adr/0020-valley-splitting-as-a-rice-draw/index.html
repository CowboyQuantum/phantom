    0020 — Valley splitting is a Rice draw per dot from alloy disorder, with a deterministic part for MOS; three signatures

    Status: Accepted Date: 2026-10-26 Step: 5

    Context

    Silicon dots have two low-lying valleys whose splitting varies from dot to dot and sets whether Pauli spin blockade works and where spin relaxation has hot spots. Atomistic calculation is out of scope (plan §3). The alloy-disorder theory of Si/SiGe wells gives a statistical description that is both simple and the one the current literature uses to compare devices.

    Decision

    • Distribution (valley.splitting): the valley coupling is a complex number Δ_det + σ(n₁ + i n₂) with n₁, n₂ standard normal; the splitting |Δ| follows a Rice distribution. σ scales as 1/ℓ with the dot radius, as √(x̄(1 − x̄)) with the germanium concentration the electron samples, and as the square root of the interface width (overlap with the alloy); Δ_det is small for SiGe and, for MOS, large and linear in the vertical field with a smaller random part from roughness. Reference values are placeholders (calibration: none) until paqueletwuetz2022-valley-alloy, losert2023-valley-strategies and yang2013-mos-valley are confirmed; the P1 row sets σ and Δ_det from the measured distribution.
    • Interface steps are not modelled explicitly (open decision of the step file resolved for now): a step_factor hook scales σ, so a later step model can plug in without changing the draw's shape.
    • Signatures (valley.signatures): the second electron's addition energy rises as +½ g μ_B B until g μ_B B = E_V and falls after (magnetospectroscopy kink at B* = E_V / g μ_B); the spin relaxation rate has a Lorentzian hot spot where E_Z = E_V on a background that the spin layer will make field-dependent; pulsed-gate spectroscopy splits a transition by the pulse amplitude and adds a weaker excited-state line E_V / α away (idealised, fixed weight).
    • Per-dot draws take the dot's harmonic radius from electrostatics.dots; compile (Step 7) will attach them to the compiled device, and spin (Step 6) consumes them for PSB.

    Consequences

    • Easier: a device's valley splittings are draws with the right dot-to-dot spread and the right size and material dependence; the fraction of dots unusable for PSB falls out of the distribution.
    • Harder: the deterministic part and the absolute scale are placeholders; interface steps are a hook.
    • Must now: P0 tests for the scalings and the Rayleigh/Rice limits; protocols valley-splitting-draws, magnetospectroscopy-transition, t1-hot-spot, pulsed-gate-spectroscopy.

    Alternatives considered

    • A fixed splitting per material — rejected: ADR 0002, and PSB statistics are the point.
    • Tight-binding valley calculations — out of scope by plan §3.
